# First AI Use Case: How to Pick One That Actually Ships

Your **first AI use case** should not be “we installed ChatGPT.” It should be one painful, repeated workflow where a model can classify, extract, or draft—and a human still owns the risky bits. This guide gives a scorecard, anti-patterns, and a 30-day path for mid-market and founder-led teams who know AI matters but freeze at where to start.

## Why “first use case” is the real strategy problem

Most organisations do not fail because the model is weak. They fail because:

- Nobody owns the process after the demo  
- There is no baseline (cycle time, error rate, hours)  
- Data lives in inboxes and PDFs with no access path  
- Success was defined as “people tried the tool,” not a business metric  

Picking the first AI use case is therefore a *portfolio and risk* decision, not a feature shop. If you have not yet mapped organisational constraints, skim an [AI readiness assessment](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) in parallel—do not use readiness as an excuse to never ship.

## The five-factor scorecard

Score each candidate 1–5. Prefer the highest total that a **single owner** can pilot.

| Factor | Ask | High score looks like |
|--------|-----|------------------------|
| **Volume** | How often does this run weekly? | Dozens+ of similar items |
| **Pain** | Who complains if it is slow or wrong? | Clear cost or delay |
| **Data** | Can we access examples safely? | Exportable or API-accessible |
| **Error cost** | What if the model is wrong? | Recoverable with review |
| **Owner** | Who fixes it on day 31? | Named role, not “AI committee” |

**Rule of thumb:** if any factor scores 1, it is not a first use case—it is a science project.

### Strong first-use-case patterns

1. **Document / email intake** — extract fields, flag missing data, queue exceptions.  
2. **Support triage** — classify intent and urgency; route; draft replies for common intents.  
3. **CRM hygiene** — summarise calls/emails into structured fields.  
4. **Internal knowledge handoff** — pack context for the next team with fewer missed fields.

These map cleanly onto [AI workflow automation](/blog/ai-workflow-automation) once you have a winner.

### Weak first-use-case patterns (avoid)

- Site-wide chatbot with no FAQ owner and no deflection metric  
- Fully autonomous refunds, credit, or medical/legal advice  
- “Rewrite the whole ERP with agents”  
- Buying five tools before one process map exists  
- Use cases that depend on data you are not allowed to send to a vendor

## Worked example: three candidates, one winner

Imagine a 40-person services firm:

| Candidate | Volume | Pain | Data | Error cost | Owner | Total |
|-----------|--------|------|------|------------|-------|-------|
| Public website chatbot | 3 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 1 | **12** |
| Invoice PDF field extraction | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | **23** |
| Fully auto quote pricing | 3 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 3 | **13** |

Invoice extraction wins: high volume, clear owner in finance, and human review on exceptions. The chatbot loses on ownership; auto-pricing loses on error cost.

## 30-day pilot for the first AI use case

### Week 1 — Baseline and design
- Count volume and median cycle time for the last 4 weeks  
- Collect 50–100 real examples (redact if needed)  
- Define human gates and “done” state  
- Write success criteria (e.g. −30% handling time, ≤X% exception rate)

### Week 2–3 — Thin build
- One trigger → model step → write-back → log  
- Prefer structured outputs over free-form chat  
- Sample review every day; track disagreements  

### Week 4 — Decide
Scale only if metrics moved and someone will own monitoring. Otherwise kill or redesign—do not expand to five departments on vibes.

For stack patterns and orchestration choices, use the [AI workflow automation](/blog/ai-workflow-automation) guide. For broader rollout after the pilot, see the [small-business AI implementation playbook](/blog/ai_automation_for_small_business_owners_2025_implementation_playbook).

## NZ / mid-market notes

- **Privacy:** invoices and tickets often contain personal data—check vendor terms and retention.  
- **Systems:** Xero, HubSpot, Intercom, and shared drives are common sources; API access beats screenshot bots.  
- **Capacity:** fractional technical leadership is fine for architecture review; day-to-day ownership still needs an internal process owner.

## Connect the dots (do not expand random LEGACY themes)

Once the first AI use case ships:

1. Document the runbook and metrics  
2. Add adjacent workflows in the same domain (same data, same owner)  
3. Revisit strategy and [AI readiness](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) gaps that blocked other candidates  
4. Only then open “build vs buy” conversations for custom systems  

Do not let a successful invoice pilot justify a random SAP or fractional-CTO content binge that is off your Primary strategy.

## FAQ

### What is a good first AI use case?
A good first AI use case is a real, repeated workflow with measurable volume, available data, an owner, and recoverable errors under human review—not a flashy chat demo with no baseline metrics.

### How do I choose my first AI project?
Score candidates on volume, data quality, error cost, integration access, and business owner. Pick the highest score that a single team can pilot in 30 days without a programme office.

### What first AI use cases fail most often?
Company-wide chatbots with no process owner, fully autonomous decisions on high-stakes outcomes, and tools bought before the process is mapped all fail more often than narrow intake or triage pilots.

### How long should a first AI pilot take?
Plan a 30-day thin slice with a baseline, human-in-the-loop, and a kill/scale decision. Stretching pilots for months usually means scope or ownership is wrong.

### Do I need an AI readiness assessment first?
A lightweight readiness pass helps when data, skills, or governance are unknown. If one workflow already has clean data and a willing owner, you can pilot in parallel with a light readiness check.

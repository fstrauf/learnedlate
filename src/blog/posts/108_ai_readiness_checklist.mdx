# AI Readiness Checklist: 12 Questions Before You Spend a Dollar

An **AI readiness checklist** is a scored set of yes/no questions across four areas — data, workflows, people, and risk — that tells you whether your business can absorb an AI project this quarter, or which foundation to fix first. Run it in 20 minutes. If you score under 9 of 12, the honest answer is not "buy a platform" — it is "fix the foundation."

This is the fast screen. For the deeper diagnostic — data audit, workflow mapping, and a prioritised roadmap — pair it with the [AI readiness assessment](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment). For what to do with a passing score, see [where to start with AI](/blog/where-to-start-with-ai).

## Why a checklist beats a strategy deck

Most "AI strategy" work dies because it starts at the wrong end: tools, vendors, and vision statements. Readiness starts at your end: what you have, who owns it, and what could break. A 12-question checklist forces the conversation onto evidence. It also gives you a number you can re-score in 90 days — which is the only kind of AI progress that is real.

## The 12 questions

Score each yes or no. No partial credit — "sort of" is no.

### Data (questions 1–3)

1. **Named.** You can name the system of record for the data your first AI use case needs — it is not "spreadsheet + inbox + memory."
2. **Clean.** That data has an owner who fixes errors when they are found, not a shared drive everyone dreads.
3. **Permitted.** You know whether that data may legally leave your systems (customer contracts, privacy obligations), and the answer is written down.

### Workflows (questions 4–6)

4. **Documented.** The workflow you most want to speed up is written down step by step — someone new could follow it.
5. **Measured.** You know the baseline: how long it takes and what "done right" looks like, in numbers.
6. **Bounded.** The workflow has a clear start and end — it is a process, not "operations" or "marketing."

### People (questions 7–9)

7. **Owner.** One named person will be accountable for the AI version of the workflow, not a committee.
8. **Reviewer.** A human will check AI output before it reaches a customer, and they have time budgeted to do it.
9. **Executor.** The person doing the workflow today is willing to change how they work — they asked for help, not had it imposed.

### Risk (questions 10–12)

10. **Accuracy stakes.** If the AI gets an answer wrong, you know what the damage is and it is not regulatory, legal, or safety-critical.
11. **Vendor exit.** You can name what you lose and how you replace it if the AI vendor doubles their price or shuts down.
12. **Outcome.** The goal is a measured business outcome (hours saved, faster turnaround, fewer errors) — not "adopt AI."

## Score it

| Yes answers | Verdict | Next move |
|---|---|---|
| 10–12 | Ready | Pick one workflow, run a 30-day thin slice, measure before/after. |
| 7–9 | Close | Fix your no answers first — usually data permission or the review step. Re-score in 30 days. |
| 4–6 | Not yet | Foundations first. The [readiness assessment](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) maps what to fix in what order. |
| 0–3 | Stop | Do not buy tools. Your problem is operational clarity, and AI will multiply the confusion. |

Most NZ small businesses we talk to land at 5–7. That is normal — and it is exactly why "we tried ChatGPT and it didn't stick" is so common. The tool was never the blocker.

## A worked example

A 12-person professional services firm scored 7. Their no answers: data permission (question 3 — client contracts silent on AI tooling), measurement (question 5 — no baseline for proposal drafting), and vendor exit (question 11). The fix cost nothing: a contract addendum, a two-week manual time log, and a decision to prefer tools with data export. They re-scored 10, ran a 30-day proposal-drafting slice, and cut proposal turnaround from 6 days to 2. None of that required an AI platform purchase — it required the checklist.

## What "ready" does not mean

Ready does not mean you need custom software. Most businesses that pass 9 of 12 are best served by assembling existing tools — the [build vs buy decision](/blog/ai-build-vs-buy) is a separate threshold, and "ready" just means you can now have that conversation with evidence instead of enthusiasm. It also does not mean every workflow is ready. Readiness is per-workflow: you might be ready to automate proposal drafts and nowhere near ready to touch client reporting.

## Where the checklist leads

A passing score points at one next step, not ten: pick a single workflow with a named owner and run a thin slice. The [first AI use case](/blog/first-ai-use-case) scorecard covers selection. If your no answers cluster in people or review, an [AI implementation partner](/blog/ai-implementation-partner) is usually cheaper than a failed in-house platform build. And if the workflow is repetitive, document-driven, and rules-based, [AI workflow automation](/blog/ai-workflow-automation) is the natural first slice.

## FAQ

**What is an AI readiness checklist?**
A short scored questionnaire — typically 10–15 yes/no questions — covering data quality, workflow documentation, team capability, and risk constraints. It tells you whether your business can absorb an AI project now, or which foundation to fix first.

**How do you assess AI readiness?**
Score four areas: data, workflows, people, and risk. Under 75% yes answers means fix foundations before tooling.

**What are the signs a business is NOT ready for AI?**
Nobody can name the workflow they want to change, data lives in inboxes with no owner, there is no human review step planned, and the goal is "adopt AI" rather than a measured outcome.

**AI readiness checklist vs AI readiness assessment — what is the difference?**
A checklist is the fast scored screen you run yourself in 20 minutes. An assessment is the deeper diagnostic — interviews, data audit, and a prioritised roadmap. Run the checklist first.

**Does business size change what ready means?**
No — a 5-person trade business and a 200-person services firm answer the same 12 questions. What changes is who owns the answers.

An **AI implementation partner** is not a vendor who renames “digital transformation” and adds ChatGPT to the deck. It is a delivery team that puts models **inside** a real process—integrations, human review, evaluation, and change management—so the pilot becomes operations.

Mid-market and founder-led teams (including NZ/ANZ operators) usually do not fail for lack of tools. They fail because nobody owns the workflow, data is stuck across systems, and “AI projects” end as demos. This guide is how to decide if you need a partner, how to select one, and what a 90-day engagement should look like.

## What an AI implementation partner actually does

| They do | They do not |
|---------|-------------|
| Ship one workflow end-to-end | Deliver only a strategy PDF |
| Wire AI into systems of record | Hand you a prompt pack and leave |
| Define metrics and eval samples | Optimise vanity chatbot demos |
| Design human-in-the-loop | Promise fully autonomous everything on day one |
| Transfer ownership to your team | Lock you into perpetual mystery retainers |

If you are earlier on the ladder, start with an [AI readiness assessment](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) and a [first AI use case](/blog/first-ai-use-case) before you hire anyone.

## When you need a partner (and when you do not)

**Hire a partner when:**

1. The use case is scored and named (volume, owner, error cost).  
2. Build-vs-buy already points to assemble or a narrow custom slice (off-the-shelf failed a real pilot).  
3. Internal engineers are maxed, or you lack integration/eval experience.  
4. You need production write-back in **90 days**, not a lab.

**Stay in-house when:**

- You only need a SaaS feature turned on (helpdesk AI, CRM copilot).  
- No owner will spend 4–8 hours a week on the process.  
- You have not measured a baseline—partners cannot fix undefined work.

**Do not hire** for “explore AI for the business” with no workflow. That is a workshop, not implementation.

## Selection scorecard

Score candidates 1–5 on each row. Prefer total ≥ 28 / 40.

| Criterion | Strong signal | Weak signal |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| **Workflow bias** | Talks process, metrics, failure modes | Talks models and platforms first |
| **Integration proof** | Shows write-back to CRM/ERP/email | Screenshots of chat UIs only |
| **Evals** | Sample sets, precision/recall language | “We’ll tune prompts until it feels good” |
| **Change management** | Training, runbooks, kill criteria | Go-live with no adoption plan |
| **Commercial shape** | Fixed thin-slice + optional expand | Open SOW with vague “AI roadmap” |
| **Domain fit** | Relevant industry or systems | Pure generic gen-AI agency |
| **Security** | Data handling, retention, SSO story | Hand-wavy “enterprise grade” |
| **Handover** | Your team can run it after exit | Forever dependency by design |

Ask for **one** case study with before/after metrics (cycle time, error rate, hours saved). If they cannot share even anonymised numbers, treat marketing as unproven.

## Engagement shape that ships

### Phase 0 — Alignment (1 week)

- Confirm workflow, systems, success metric, owner  
- Data access checklist  
- Risk and human-review policy  

### Phase 1 — Thin slice (weeks 2–6)

- Build the minimum path: trigger → model step → validation → write-back  
- 50–100 labelled examples for evaluation  
- Weekly demo on **production-like** data, not a sandbox toy  

### Phase 2 — Harden (weeks 7–10)

- Monitoring, failure queues, permissions  
- Runbooks for operators  
- Compare to baseline; decide kill / iterate / scale  

### Phase 3 — Handover (weeks 11–12)

- Code/config ownership, docs, training  
- Optional light retainer for support—not a second roadmap by stealth  

This mirrors solid [AI workflow automation](/blog/ai-workflow-automation) practice: models inside processes, not chat as a product.

## Commercial models (pick the boring one)

| Model | Use when | Watch-outs |
|-------|----------|------------|
| **Fixed thin-slice** | First workflow | Scope creep into “also this other process” |
| **Time & materials cap** | Unknown integration risk | Needs weekly burn reports |
| **Outcome bonus** | Clear $ metric | Gaming metrics; define measurement up front |
| **Retainer** | After something is live | Avoid retainers with no shipped process |

Prefer a **capped first engagement** with an explicit kill date. Multi-year “AI transformation” retainers without a live workflow are usually strategy theatre.

## Red flags (walk away)

1. Leads with model brands instead of your process map.  
2. Wants six months of discovery before a pilot.  
3. No talk of evaluation, human review, or write-back.  
4. Pushes custom training when assemble would work.  
5. Refuses a kill/scale gate after the first slice.  
6. Staffs juniors only after the sales pitch.  
7. Claims guaranteed rankings or guaranteed ROI without a baseline.

## What you must own as the client

Partners cannot replace:

- **A process owner** with authority to change the workflow  
- **Access** to systems and sample data  
- **A decision** on error cost (what the model is allowed to get wrong)  
- **Change communication** to the people who do the work today  

If those are missing, fix them before the SOW—or the engagement becomes expensive theatre.

## Partner vs product vs internal team

| Need | Best default |
|------|--------------|
| Turn on vendor AI in a tool you already pay for | Product + internal owner |
| Multi-system automation, clear use case, thin team | Implementation partner |
| Long-term proprietary product | Internal team (+ partner for first 90 days optional) |
| “We should do something with AI” | Readiness + use-case workshop—not a partner SOW |

## RFP questions worth asking

1. Which workflow will be live in 90 days, and what is out of scope?  
2. How do you measure quality (eval set size, human review rate)?  
3. Which systems will you write to, and who owns API risk?  
4. What does week-6 demo look like if access is delayed?  
5. What happens if metrics miss target—iterate plan or exit?  
6. Who on our team will be able to operate this without you?

## Bottom line

A good **AI implementation partner** ships a scoped workflow with metrics and handover. Hire them when the use case is real and capacity is the bottleneck—not when you need a vision deck. Start with readiness and a first use case, run build-vs-buy honestly, then buy delivery help for the thin slice that matters.

If you only remember one filter: **ask what goes live in 90 days and how you will know it worked.** Anything else is optional.

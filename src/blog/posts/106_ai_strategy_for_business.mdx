# AI Strategy for Business: Decide Outcomes Before You Buy Seats

An **AI strategy for business** is four written decisions: which outcomes matter this quarter, which workflows are allowed to change, whether you buy / assemble / build, and who owns the keep/kill call. It is not a vendor shortlist, a model bake-off, or a 40-slide “AI vision.” If those four decisions are not on one page, you do not have a strategy—you have a shopping habit.

This page is the mid-market operator version. It pairs with a [readiness assessment](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) (can we even run a slice?) and a [first AI use case](/blog/first-ai-use-case) scorecard (which process goes first). It is *not* the older [90-day small-business action plan](/blog/2025-06-22-ai-strategy-small-business-90-day-action-plan)—that is a delivery calendar once the decisions exist.

## Why most “AI strategies” do not change anything

Strategy decks fail in predictable ways:

| Deck says | Missing decision | Result after 90 days |
|-----------|------------------|----------------------|
| “We will be AI-first” | No outcome metric | Seats, no process change |
| “Explore use cases” | No in/out list | Fifteen pilots, zero keepers |
| “Select a platform” | No buy/assemble/build rule | Custom build for a generic job |
| “Upskill the company” | No owner on a workflow | Training with nothing to apply it to |

A usable **AI strategy for business** is closer to a portfolio rule than a transformation narrative. You are choosing *where models are allowed to touch work*, and *what “good” means* before anyone signs a statement of work.

## The one-page strategy (fill this in first)

Copy this. If a cell is blank, you are not ready to buy.

| Decision | Write it in one sentence |
|----------|--------------------------|
| **Outcomes (≤3)** | e.g. cut quote-cycle time; reduce missed fields on intake |
| **In-scope workflows** | Named processes with weekly volume |
| **Out of scope (this quarter)** | e.g. unsupervised customer decisions; replacing the ERP |
| **Buy / assemble / build** | Default rule + who can override it |
| **Data & review** | What may leave the current system; who reviews outputs |
| **Owner** | Named role for keep/kill on day 30 and day 90 |
| **90-day calendar** | Slice 1 / decision / slice 2 (or stop) |

That page *is* the strategy. Everything else is commentary.

### Worked example (regional services firm)

- **Outcomes:** reduce time-to-quote; cut rework from missing job fields  
- **In scope:** inbound email → job record; quote draft from a site visit note  
- **Out of scope:** public chatbot; pricing the job without a human  
- **Path:** assemble (inbox + existing job system + human approve). Do not build.  
- **Review:** coordinator checks every extracted field for 30 days  
- **Owner:** operations lead  
- **Calendar:** days 1–30 intake extract; day 30 keep/kill; days 31–90 quote draft *only if* intake kept

Notice there is no model name on the page. Model choice is an implementation detail after the workflow is named.

## Strategy is not implementation (do not invert them)

| Layer | Question it answers | Typical artefact |
|-------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Strategy** | Which outcomes and workflows this quarter? | The one-pager above |
| **Readiness** | Will data, skills, or governance block a slice? | Six-dimension [readiness](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) if unknown |
| **Use-case pick** | Which process goes first? | [First use case](/blog/first-ai-use-case) scorecard |
| **Implementation** | How does the thin slice run? | [Workflow automation](/blog/ai-workflow-automation) pilot + review log |
| **Build vs buy** | Rent, stitch, or write software? | [Build vs buy](/blog/ai-build-vs-buy) scorecard |
| **Partner** | Do we need outside delivery? | [Implementation partner](/blog/ai-implementation-partner) only after a failed or capacity-blocked slice |

If someone proposes a platform *before* the one-pager exists, that is procurement, not strategy.

For a small team that already knows the first process, skip the long workshop and run the 30-day slice. The [small-business 90-day plan](/blog/2025-06-22-ai-strategy-small-business-90-day-action-plan) is a pacing tool for that case—not a second strategy document.

## Buy, assemble, or build — put the rule in the strategy

Leave this as a default so every new idea does not restart the architecture war:

1. **Buy** when the job is generic and the data already lives in a product (CRM copilot, support draft, meeting notes).  
2. **Assemble** when two or more systems must hand off and a human can still approve. This is where most real **AI strategy for business** value sits.  
3. **Build** only when the process is a durable edge, buy failed a real pilot, and a named owner can run a 90-day delivery slice.

Write the override: *who* is allowed to open a build, and *what evidence* they need (failed buy, volume × value, write-back requirement). Without that rule, every vendor meeting becomes a new strategy.

## Governance belongs in the strategy—but as constraints, not a programme

You do not need a 40-page policy to start. You do need three constraints on the one-pager:

- What data may be sent to a vendor (and what must stay in-system)  
- Which outputs require a human before they reach a customer or a ledger  
- Who is accountable when the model is wrong  

If those constraints are politically hard, that is [AI governance strategy](/blog/ai-governance-strategy) work—still a constraint set, not a reason to skip naming outcomes.

## A 90-day strategy calendar (not a transformation)

**Days 1–10 — decisions.** Fill the one-pager. Kill anything that has no owner.

**Days 11–40 — first slice.** Baseline, human-reviewed pilot, write-back the way you already work. This is implementation under a strategy, not a new strategy.

**Day 40 — keep / kill / scale.** Written. If kill, pick the next in-scope workflow or stop. Do not “expand the platform.”

**Days 41–90 — second slice or stop.** Only if slice 1 kept. Same owner model. If the team cannot run two slices, you have a capacity problem—consider a partner, or shrink scope. Do not add a third outcome.

Teams that skip day 40 always end up with five half-alive tools and no strategy to retire them.

## When to get outside help (and when not to)

**Stay internal** when one workflow is obvious, data is exportable, and an owner has four hours a week.

**Bring a [strategy / implementation conversation](/services/strategy)** when:

- Leadership cannot agree on outcomes  
- Every department has a pet use case and none will yield  
- Buy vs build is stuck as a religious argument  
- You need a first slice designed so the internal team can run the second one

Do not hire a partner to “write the AI strategy” as a 60-page PDF with no named workflow. That is how you buy another deck.

## FAQ

**What is an AI strategy for business?**  
An AI strategy for business is a written set of decisions: which outcomes matter this quarter, which workflows are allowed to change, whether you buy, assemble, or build, and who owns the keep/kill call. It is not a tool list or a 40-slide vision deck.

**How do you create an AI strategy for a business?**  
Name two or three outcomes, pick one or two workflows that move those outcomes, run a 30-day thin slice on the first workflow, then decide keep/kill/scale. Expand only after you have a before/after number.

**What should an AI strategy include?**  
Outcomes and metrics, in-scope and out-of-scope workflows, a buy/assemble/build rule, data and review constraints, owners, and a 90-day calendar. If any of those is missing, you have a wish list.

**AI strategy vs AI implementation — what is the difference?**  
Strategy chooses the outcomes and the first workflows. Implementation is the delivery of a thin slice (baseline, human review, write-back). Most failed programmes invert that order: they implement a platform, then hunt for a strategy.

**Do small businesses need a formal AI strategy?**  
They need the decisions, not the theatre. A one-page strategy plus a 30-day pilot beats a 90-day planning cycle with no owner. A dated 90-day small-business action plan is a delivery calendar, not a substitute for choosing outcomes.
